Flashcard App – Requirement 02
Learner Memory, Scheduling & Recall Selection
Mneme Foundation v2.3 / MVP Contract v1.2 aligned — Learning Profile

## 1. Purpose
Define the Learning Profile pipeline from durable learning history to model-specific learner-state estimates, scheduling policy, and RecallForm/activity selection while keeping current flashcard behavior simple and offline-first. These learner-memory semantics do not define Agent Memory retrieval/ranking.

## 2. Canonical decision pipeline
LearningEpisode/LearningEvent → shared HistoryProjector/normalizer → EvaluationOutcome/MemoryEvidence → LearnerMemoryModel → predictions/state → SchedulingPolicy → KnowledgeUnit/target selection → RecallForm selector. Prediction is not policy; WHEN and HOW remain separate decisions.

## 3. Learner state
Learner state is latent, model-specific, uncertain when the model has meaningful uncertainty, and rebuildable. Unknown/no evidence is distinct from weak/forgotten. Overall mastery is a convenience projection, never source of truth.

## 4. MVP strategies
FixedIntervalStrategy remains a first-class compatibility strategy. A local interpretable adaptive strategy may use Again/Hard/Good/Easy plus elapsed time and history. AI is not required for scheduling. Strategy selection belongs to learner/app configuration, not Knowledge.

## 5. Scheduling policy
Scheduling consumes learner-model predictions plus policy inputs such as importance, desired retention, workload/time budget, goal/deadline, and product constraints. A single scalar score must not be assumed to express all policy value; policies may use hard constraints, reserved categories/quotas, then ranking inside candidate groups.

## 6. Resource budgets
Future/general scheduling should reason about estimated learning opportunity cost/time rather than fixed item counts alone. MVP may keep simple daily limits but interfaces should not make “N cards” the only budget representation.

## 7. RecallForm selection
The selector chooses HOW to test a due/selected target. It may prefer forms that generate evidence for weak or under-observed targets while respecting exposure contamination and sibling suppression. RecallForm identity does not itself define a unique memory trace.

## 8. Exposure contamination
If a prior event/form exposed information required by a later form, that later performance must be marked/discounted according to policy rather than treated as independent unaided retrieval. System-provided information origin is therefore input to evidence/state reduction.

## 9. Response time, hints and self-rating
Response time, hint usage, skips, retries and Again/Hard/Good/Easy are observations/signals. They do not directly mutate a universal mastery variable. Their interpretation belongs to EvidenceMapping/MemoryModel.

## 10. History projection and incremental replay
Memory models and schedulers must consume a shared normalized historical projection rather than independently decoding event payloads. Repository APIs must permit bounded reads (since boundary/by target/by episode) so replay can later start from a compatible ReplayCheckpoint instead of loading all history into memory.

## 11. ReplayCheckpoint reservation
A future ReplayCheckpoint records model definition/version, parameters, state snapshot, history-through boundary, and the semantic dependencies required to interpret that snapshot. Checkpoints accelerate replay and are disposable/rebuildable relative to canonical history. MVP may initially operate without persisted checkpoints.

## 12. Re-inference versus rescheduling
Rebuilding learner state with a new model does not automatically rewrite historical schedule decisions or force immediate rescheduling. Re-inference and future scheduling are separate operations.

## 13. Local-first and async enrichment
Next-review decisions required for normal use must be possible from the local store. Remote model fitting, embeddings, cloud sync, or AI diagnostics can run asynchronously/fail open without blocking a review.

## 14. Diagnostics boundary
Repeated failure, leech-like patterns, low evidence coverage, contamination, or uncertainty may produce diagnostics. Diagnostics are derived views and must not become authoritative learner history.

## 15. Legacy migration
Legacy per-card due/interval data migrates into the FixedInterval strategy/current schedule state. Existing historical review records are migrated only to the extent observable fields exist; missing semantics remain unknown rather than invented.

## 16. Acceptance criteria
FixedInterval reproduces legacy behavior; adaptive strategy can be swapped without changing canonical history; unknown stays unknown; recent answer exposure can suppress/discount sibling forms; scheduler works offline; learner state rebuild yields equivalent results for same history/model/config; history is never semantically deduplicated.

## 17. Dual-Profile Boundary (v2.3)
Within the dual-profile architecture, `MemoryModel` in this requirement is learner-specific and SHOULD be exposed as `LearnerMemoryModel` or namespaced `learning.MemoryModel`. Agent Memory has retrieval/ranking/consolidation policies rather than a learner-state model or spaced-review scheduler.
- Prediction != scheduling remains a Learning Profile invariant.
- AgentContextProjection budgets are analogous engineering projections but are not learning schedules and do not produce MemoryEvidence.
- Shared retrieval/index infrastructure may be reused, but semantic search never substitutes for structured learner state or stable Knowledge identity.
